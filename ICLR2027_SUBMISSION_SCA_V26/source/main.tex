\begin{abstract}
During training, split-latent generators often reconstruct an observation from
content and style codes encoded from that same observation; at generation,
their decoder inputs may instead be sampled independently.  We formalize this
train--generation shift as
\emph{sampler compatibility} between the encoded conditional joint
$Q_y=q(\zc,\zs\mid y)$ and the declared sampling law
$P_y=p(\zc\mid y)p(\zs)$.  An exact expected-conditional KL identity decomposes incompatibility into
style-prior mismatch, condition information in style, content-prior mismatch,
and within-condition code dependence.  We pair a direct joint test with
clause-specific diagnostics, decoder interventions, and task metrics, thereby
separating latent compatibility, decoded transport, and generation competence.
An exact-marginal counterexample and controlled audits on image and known-factor
data show that aggregate matching can coexist with strong leakage and decoded
style-donor control.  In three fully traceable matched MNIST pairs, a
\emph{factorized-contract training} (\FACT{}) intervention reduces the selected
joint-MMD diagnostic by $38.9\!\pm\!3.6\%$ and style-probe accuracy from
$76.9\%$ to $11.1\%$, but lowers generation accuracy by
$9.9\!\pm\!2.8$ points; a post-specified energy distance does not improve in
every pair.  Thus aggregate matching is only one clause of independent
recombination, and improving a chosen compatibility diagnostic need not improve
generation competence.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Many split-latent generators are trained and sampled in different ways.  During
reconstruction, content and style codes come from the same observation.  During
generation, the two codes are drawn or recombined independently.  If the
decoder has learned dependencies that occur only in paired codes, generation
queries it under a latent distribution it did not observe during reconstruction.

Let $Q_y=q(\zc,\zs\mid y)$ denote the encoded pair conditional on the requested
condition $y$, and let $P_y=p(\zc\mid y)p(\zs)$ denote the independently sampled
decoder-input law.  We call $Q_y=P_y$ \emph{sampler compatibility}.  Throughout,
``content'' means the code intended to carry the requested condition and
``style'' means the designated residual code; these are operational roles, not
universal perceptual definitions.  The contract applies only when a model uses
independent recombination.  A generator that models and samples $Q_y$ directly
makes no such distributional substitution.

Aggregate prior matching checks only one projection of this joint claim.  It
may make $q(\zs)$ close to a Gaussian without ensuring that style is invariant
to $y$, that encoded content follows its sampling prior, or that the two codes
are independent within each condition.  Good reconstruction and a small global
prior discrepancy can therefore coexist with $Q_y\ne P_y$.

A failure of sampler compatibility is not, by itself, a quality verdict.  A decoder can be insensitive to the
mismatched directions, while matched latent laws do not guarantee realistic or
task-correct outputs.  We consequently distinguish three questions: latent
\emph{compatibility} ($Q_y=P_y$), \emph{transport compatibility}
($G_\#Q_y=G_\#P_y$), and \emph{generation competence} relative to the real
conditional data law and intended task.  MNIST swaps show a mismatch that the
decoder actively uses; a separate SCFlow boundary case shows why endpoint and
task evidence must still be interpreted in the native model space.

Our technical contribution is not a new KL rule: it is the sampler-specific
reference joint and the resulting audit target.  The decomposition turns
familiar marginal diagnostics into necessary but individually insufficient
clauses, adds content-prior and conditional block-dependence requirements, and
separates latent failure from decoded consequence.  We ask whether aggregate
matching can conceal incompatibility, whether the decoder uses that mismatch,
and what changes when the diagnosed clauses are optimized directly.

Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We formulate the declared generation law as a falsifiable joint
    contract, derive an exact four-term KL decomposition, and distinguish latent
    compatibility, decoded transport, and task competence.
    \item We operationalize a direct joint equality test followed by clause-specific
    attribution and decoder/task interventions; the diagnostics test zero-sets
    rather than claim to estimate the four KL magnitudes.
    \item Across exact constructions, controlled image models, Shapes3D, and a
    released-checkpoint boundary case, we show that the audit distinguishes
    null-like and consequential failures.  A fully traceable matched intervention
    then exposes both diagnostic gains and their generation cost.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Aggregate matching and latent decomposition.}
Aggregate-prior metrics examine one marginal; leakage probes examine whether
a designated representation predicts a condition; factor scores describe
selected cross-loadings.  Each is useful, but none alone tests the conditional
joint substituted at generation.  This distinction connects several familiar
objectives.  VAEs~\citep{kingma2013autoencoding} penalize per-example KL,
whereas WAEs~\citep{tolstikhin2017wasserstein} match aggregated posteriors with
objectives such as MMD~\citep{gretton2012kernel}.  The averaged VAE KL separates
information capacity from aggregate-prior mismatch~\citep{hoffman2016elbo};
$\beta$-VAE, $\beta$-TCVAE, FactorVAE, and DIP-VAE further target capacity,
unconditional total correlation, or aggregate moments
\citep{higgins2017beta,chen2018isolating,kim2018disentangling,kumar2018dipvae}.
Our reference is instead the conditional product law induced by the declared
sampler, which additionally requires a content prior and block independence
given $y$.  Aggregate matching is therefore not incorrect; it certifies one
marginal of a larger joint claim.

\paragraph{Learning content--style invariance and preventing leakage.}
Split-latent models use grouped observations, labels, paired supervision, or
latent optimization to separate specified and residual variation
\citep{mathieu2016disentangling,bouchacourt2018multilevel,jha2018cycle,ilse2020diva,gabbay2020lord}.
Leakage filtering directly probes class information in style before
recombination~\citep{ridgeway2018leakage}; fair representation, adversarial,
Fader, and information-based objectives suppress designated nuisance signals
\citep{louizos2015variational,ganin2015unsupervised,lample2017fader,moyer2018invariant}.
Recent diffusion and autoregressive systems explicitly suppress visual-prompt
or style-code content leakage, including StyleKeeper and CSD-VAR
\citep{jeong2025stylekeeper,nguyen2025csdvar}, as well as feature masking and
variance-invariance approaches~\citep{zhu2025less,wu2025variance}.  These
methods target leakage or synthesis quality.  Here condition information in
style is one possible source of $Q_y\ne P_y$; it can vanish while content-prior
mismatch or conditional code dependence remains.  More broadly, unsupervised
factor identification requires assumptions on the model or
data~\citep{locatello2019challenging}.

\paragraph{Disentanglement versus editability.}
Recent theory formalizes information-theoretic content--style separation and
editability as complementary properties, and gives identifiability and
finite-sample results for diffusion models under structural assumptions
\citep{wang2025candiffusion}.  We do not prove factor identifiability or the
existence of an editable decoder.  Instead, for a fixed trained encoder,
decoder, and declared sampler, we test whether the paired decoder-input law is
the law used for independent recombination.  Our transport check is therefore
sampler- and decoder-specific rather than an alternative definition of
editability.

\paragraph{Recombination and its audit.}
MUNIT and DRIT combine content with sampled style
\citep{huang2018munit,lee2018drit}; recent methods use variance patterns or
invertible merging to obtain content--style structure
\citep{wu2025variance,ma2025scflow}.  These methods evaluate decoded quality and
diversity under recombination.  Our complementary question is whether the
encoded conditional joint equals the particular input law used by that
recombination, which clauses differ if it does not, and whether the difference
survives decoding.

\paragraph{Known-factor evaluation.}
Class labels are only one semantic partition.  Controlled datasets such as
Shapes3D expose shape, color, scale, and orientation factors and have been
used to evaluate whether representation coordinates track known generative
variables~\citep{higgins2017beta,locatello2019challenging,deepmind2018shapes3d}.
We use them differently: a designated content/style split is tested in both
directions, with family-wise corrected dependence tests and pixel-space swap
evaluation.

\section{Factorized Sampling and Sampler Compatibility}
\label{sec:theory}

We now formalize the distributional replacement performed by factorized
sampling and derive the conditions under which it disappears.

Let $q(y)$ denote the empirical distribution of an externally specified
condition and let an encoder induce $q(\zc,\zs\mid y)$.  We condition on the
requested $y$ throughout; matching a separate sampling law $p(y)$ is outside
this contract and would add $\KL(q(y)\|p(y))$ to a full-joint comparison.  A
factorized class-conditional sampler draws
\begin{equation}
    \zc\sim p(\zc\mid y),\qquad \zs\sim p(\zs),
    \label{eq:sampler}
\end{equation}
independently.  In our case $p(\zs)=\mathcal N(0,I)$.  Many aggregate
objectives constrain only
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal L_{\mathrm{style}}=D\!\left(q(\zs),p(\zs)\right),
    \quad q(\zs)=\sum_y q(y)q(\zs\mid y),
    \label{eq:global-style}
\end{equation}

For the remainder, write
\begin{equation}
Q_y:=q(\zc,\zs\mid y),\qquad
P_y:=p(\zc\mid y)p(\zs),\qquad
\boxed{\text{sampler compatibility: }Q_y=P_y\quad q(y)\text{-a.e.}}
\label{eq:compatibility}
\end{equation}

Equation~\ref{eq:sampler} covers generators that draw both codes from learned
priors.  Translation systems that deliberately retain content from an input
instantiate the same analysis with that encoded-content law in place of
$p(\zc\mid y)$; term 3 is therefore sampler-specific rather than silently
assumed.  Our direct term-3 experiment concerns F-CS-WAE, whose stated sampler
does draw $p(\zc\mid y)$.

\subsection{Compatibility, transport, and competence}

The relevant object is not either marginal in isolation, but the discrepancy
between $Q_y$ and $P_y$.  For a possibly stochastic decoder Markov kernel
$G(dx\mid\zc,\zs)$, \emph{transport compatibility} is
$G_\#Q_y=G_\#P_y$.  Let $R_y=p_{\rm data}(x\mid y)$ denote the real conditional
data law.  \emph{Generation competence} asks whether $G_\#P_y$ agrees with
$R_y$ under task-relevant fidelity, diversity, and condition-retention metrics.
We test the latent equality first, then transport and competence separately.

\begin{theorem}[Factorized-sampling contract decomposition]
\label{thm:decomp}
Assume the displayed regular conditional distributions exist.  Define the
following extended non-negative quantity:
\begin{equation}
M_{\mathrm{fact}}:=\mathbb E_{q(y)}\!\left[
\KL\!\left(q(\zc,\zs\mid y)\,\|\,p(\zc\mid y)p(\zs)\right)\right].
\end{equation}
Then the following identity holds in $[0,\infty]$:
\begin{align}
M_{\mathrm{fact}}={}&
\underbrace{\KL(q(\zs)\|p(\zs))}_{\text{(1) style-prior mismatch}}
+\underbrace{I_q(\zs;y)}_{\text{(2) condition leakage}}\nonumber\\
&+\underbrace{\mathbb E_{q(y)}\KL(q(\zc\mid y)\|p(\zc\mid y))}_{\text{(3) content-prior mismatch}}
+\underbrace{I_q(\zc;\zs\mid y)}_{\text{(4) within-condition code dependence}}.
\label{eq:decomp}
\end{align}
All four terms are non-negative.  If $M_{\mathrm{fact}}<\infty$, then all four
terms are finite and Eq.~\ref{eq:decomp} is an ordinary real-valued equality.
\end{theorem}

The decomposition is an identity in KL, whereas our audit tests the zero-set
of each term rather than estimating its numerical magnitude.  The theorem
allows discrete or continuous $y$; our class-stratified MMD and within-class
HSIC implementation targets categorical conditions with repeated samples.

\begin{corollary}[Sampler compatibility implies transport compatibility]
\label{cor:valid}
$M_{\mathrm{fact}}=0$ iff
$q(\zc,\zs\mid y)=p(\zc\mid y)p(\zs)$ for $q(y)$-almost every $y$.
Moreover, for any fixed decoder Markov kernel $G$,
\begin{equation}
M_{\mathrm{dec}}:=\mathbb E_{q(y)}\KL\!\left(G_\#q(\cdot\mid y)\,\|\,
G_\#[p(\zc\mid y)p(\zs)]\right)\le M_{\mathrm{fact}}.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

The implication is one-way.  A decoder can discard mismatched latent
directions, so $M_{\mathrm{fact}}=0$ is sufficient but not necessary for
$M_{\mathrm{dec}}=0$.  Moreover, neither latent nor decoder transport
compatibility establishes agreement with real data.  Competence metrics are
therefore complementary to the latent decomposition and decoder interventions.

Exact equality is a falsifiable reference null, not by itself a practical
severity threshold.  For a pre-declared distribution-separating discrepancy
$d$ on the relevant representation space, define
\begin{equation}
\Delta_d:=\mathbb E_{q(y)}d(Q_y,P_y),\qquad
(d,\epsilon)\text{-compatibility: }\Delta_d\leq\epsilon .
\label{eq:epsilon-compatibility}
\end{equation}
The tolerance is necessarily metric-, scale-, and task-specific; we do not
claim a universal $\epsilon$.  Equality tests establish whether the zero anchor
is tenable, while raw statistics, null calibration, relative effects, and
decoder/task evidence assess practical magnitude.  For paired interventions we
report $R_d=1-\widehat\Delta_d^{\rm int}/\widehat\Delta_d^{\rm ctrl}$ when the
control estimate is positive, alongside competence rather than treating
rejection alone as an operational certificate.

Equation~\ref{eq:global-style} constrains only term 1.  Terms 2--4 concern how
aggregate mass is partitioned by the condition, whether content follows its
requested conditional prior, and whether the codes remain coupled within a
condition.  They are non-negative but not interchangeable: removing term 2
still permits the decoder to exploit term-4 dependence.  Table~\ref{tab:audit-map}
maps these clauses to held-out evidence; a direct test then checks the complete
joint equality without attributing its failure to one component.

\subsection{Why one clause cannot certify the contract}

The following minimal counterexample isolates why exact satisfaction of term
1 leaves term 2---and therefore the joint contract---unconstrained.

\begin{proposition}[No clause-1 certificate for clause 2]
\label{prop:nocert}
Let $\zs\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$ and let $\{A_k\}_{k=1}^K$ be a measurable
partition with $P(\zs\in A_k)=1/K$.  Define $y=k$ iff $\zs\in A_k$.  Then
$q(\zs)=\mathcal N(0,I)$ exactly for every marginal divergence $D$, while
$I(\zs;y)=H(y)=\log K$.
\end{proposition}

The construction is intentionally extremal, but the issue is not restricted
to discontinuous partitions.  Let $z\sim\mathcal N(0,1)$ and
$P(y=1\mid z)=\sigma(az)$.  Symmetry leaves the marginal of $z$ exactly
Gaussian, while $q(z\mid y=1)=2\phi(z)\sigma(az)$ and
$q(z\mid y=0)=2\phi(z)\sigma(-az)$ are smooth, full-support, and increasingly
distinguishable as $a$ grows.  Section~\ref{sec:synthetic} instantiates this
construction only to establish clause-wise insufficiency; the trained-model
experiments instead evaluate whether the complete audit distinguishes model
regimes.  Proofs are in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.

\section{Term-Aligned Audit and Case Study}
\label{sec:protocol}

\subsection{Contract-first, term-aligned diagnostics}

The audit follows diagnosis order: first test $Q_y=P_y$; only after rejection
localize the source.

The audit has two latent stages.  First, independently sampled encoded and
prior banks test the complete replacement without attribution:
\begin{equation}
T_{\mathrm{joint}}=\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}\omega_k
\MMD^2\!\left(q(\zc,\zs\mid k),p(\zc\mid k)p(\zs)\right),
\label{eq:direct-joint}
\end{equation}
using a product content--style kernel and within-class pooled-label
permutations.  This diagnostic was specified after the original checkpoint
audit and is applied to frozen checkpoints and test populations, without
retraining or model selection.  If it rejects,
the tests in Table~\ref{tab:audit-map} localize the difference: global MMD for
term 1; held-out probes and label-permutation HSIC for term 2; classwise
content-prior MMD for term 3; and within-class permutation HSIC for term 4.
Sampling-contract tests use stochastic posterior draws, while deterministic
representation probes use means.  Holm correction covers classwise families.
These statistics test the corresponding equality; they do not estimate the
numerical KL or mutual-information term.  Kernel coordinates, bandwidths,
weighting, and null construction are in Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}.

We also report classwise style-to-prior MMD,
$\MMD^2(q(\zs\mid y=k),p(\zs))$, as a sensitive \emph{composite} conditional
diagnostic.  It can reflect term 1, term 2, or both; global MMD separates the
aggregate-prior clause, while label-HSIC and probes specifically localize
condition information in style.  We do not identify this conditional MMD with
the numerical KL value of term 2.

The fixed audit uses 2,048 test examples and a stratified 60/20/20
probe-train/validation/test split; standardization is fit on probe training
data only.  Probe models, kernels, permutation counts, RNG separation, and
per-seed values are specified in Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}.

\subsection{Controlled stress-test instrument}

We use \ours{} as a fully instrumented stress-test model, not as a proposed
state-of-the-art generator.  Its declared sampler exposes all four contract
clauses; cross-family and known-factor experiments test whether the diagnostics
distinguish behavior beyond this instrument.  Its modified
ResNet-18 encoder produces hyperspherical content
$\zc\in\mathbb S^{63}$ and Gaussian style $\zs\in\R^{128}$ for a residual
decoder.  Class-specific Spherical Cauchy priors shape content; global MMD
matches sampled style to $\mathcal N(0,I)$.  Reconstruction, supervised
content-prior matching, aggregate content matching, and a content classifier
complete the phased 300-epoch objective.  Appendix~\ref{app:model} specifies
the sampler, architecture, coefficients, and schedule.

The historical per-class loss
$K^{-1}\sum_k\MMD^2(q(\zs\mid k),\mathcal N(0,I))$ targets conditional style
matching but leaves term 4 outside the intervention.  Its observed instability
motivates the clause-aligned intervention evaluated only after the diagnostic
and decoder evidence in Section~\ref{sec:fact-results}.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}


\paragraph{Reporting rules.}
The image audits cover MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10,
paired $-30^\circ/+30^\circ$ Rotated-MNIST, and Shapes3D.  All headline
sampling-contract diagnostics use stochastic posterior samples and held-out
splits.

Unbiased $\MMD^2$ estimates may be negative under the null and are never
clipped.  Non-rejection is interpreted at the available test power.  HSIC uses
plus-one permutation calibration, with domain-level decisions rather than
averaged $p$-values.  Shapes3D applies Holm correction separately within the
six content tests and six style tests.  Final epochs and audit populations are
fixed without selecting checkpoints on leakage metrics.

\subsection{Can aggregate matching hide joint incompatibility?}
\label{sec:synthetic}

The decomposition makes aggregate matching a clause rather than a certificate.
We first isolate that logical point by instantiating the smooth construction from
Proposition~\ref{prop:nocert} in 128 dimensions:
\begin{equation}
z\sim\mathcal N(0,I),\qquad
p(y=1\mid z)=\sigma(2\kappa z_1).
\end{equation}
The latent is sampled before the label, so $q_\kappa(z)=\mathcal N(0,I)$
exactly for every $\kappa$: term 1 is zero by the data-generating process, not
because a test fails to reject.  We use 50 replications of 2,048 samples, 499
MMD and HSIC permutations, and the same stratified 60/20/20 linear-probe
protocol as the checkpoint audit.

Probe accuracy rises from $49.58\!\pm\!1.86\%$ at $\kappa=0$ to
$92.30\!\pm\!1.51\%$ at $\kappa=16$.  HSIC rejects in $100\%$ of replications
for every $\kappa>0$, while its null rejection rate is $2\%$.  Mean
conditional MMD rises from $6.16\times10^{-6}$ to
$4.78\times10^{-4}$.  These results support the intended existential claim:
exact marginal equality can coexist with more than $90\%$ recoverable label
information.  The complete acceptance audit and sample-size sensitivity are
reported in Appendix~\ref{app:synthetic}.  This is an exact-marginal
counterexample, not a trained-model result.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=.98\textwidth]{exact_marginal_vs_leakage.png}
\caption{\textbf{Exact marginal matching does not certify condition
independence.}  By construction the same Gaussian latent sample is reused as

A sweep over $n=512$--$4096$ preserves $92.0$--$92.5\%$ probe accuracy and

expected pattern: at fixed $\kappa$, more observations stabilize rather than
strengthen the population predictive relation, while a valid level-.05 test
should remain near nominal size because the marginal null is exactly true.

\subsection{Does the audit distinguish model-family regimes?}
\label{sec:cross-model}

We next ask whether diagnostics shared across model families report leakage
indiscriminately or separate practically different regimes.  We train
native-family, common-backbone adaptations of F-CS-WAE,
DRIT~\citep{lee2018drit}, and DIVA~\citep{ilse2020diva} for 100 epochs on the
same two-domain Rotated-MNIST population.  The audited style variables are
F-CS-WAE $z_s$, DRIT's domain-specific attribute code, and DIVA's residual
$z_x$.  DRIT domains are audited separately because their attribute spaces

\subsection{Does the mismatch survive decoding?}
\label{sec:entropy}

\paragraph{Decoded semantics.}
The data-processing inequality leaves open whether a latent mismatch affects
outputs.  We therefore pair the fixed MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, and CIFAR-10 audit
with prior-sampling and donor-swap interventions.  Conditional MMD is .0160 on
MNIST and .010 on Fashion-MNIST, and all seedwise HSIC tests attain $p=1/201$.
Replacing global Gaussian style by post-hoc class styles raises MNIST Gen-ACC
from 10.47\% to 100\%; deterministic mean swaps similarly make decoded
identity follow the style donor in 99.28\% of MNIST and 63.92\% of CIFAR-10
cases.  An archived per-class intervention lowers donor following to 23.37\%
and 35.92\%, respectively, while within-class HSIC continues to reject,
consistent with suppressing term 2 but not term 4.

The variance head of F-CS-WAE often reaches its numerical floor, but a
three-seed variance-floor intervention does not fully explain the effect:
increasing posterior noise improves independent generation while substantial
MNIST label recovery remains.  The complete intervention, CIFAR-10 contrast,
legacy classwise-style result, qualitative grids, and per-level statistics are
reported in Appendices~\ref{app:entropy} and~\ref{app:full-results}.  Section
\ref{sec:fact-results} next tests whether directly optimizing the diagnosed
clauses changes both latent statistics and decoder attribution.

\subsection{Does the audit recover known generative factors?}
\label{sec:shapes3d}

On an IID Shapes3D split, every model passes reconstruction/content gates, but
F-CS-WAE style predicts shape at 99.0\% and content predicts nuisance factors
at 87.0\% on average.  The Holm-corrected pattern repeats over three seeds:
F-CS-WAE rejects all six factor tests in both views, while a learned
content--style VAE rejects only the expected complementary factors and
preserves shape at 99.7\%/99.8\% under prior/swap decoding, versus
89.0\%/88.0\% for F-CS-WAE.  Appendix~\ref{app:shapes3d} reports the second
near-null control, oracle scope, and per-factor results.

Appendix~\ref{app:scflow} separately audits SCFlow's released synthetic

\section{Clause-Aligned Intervention}
\label{sec:fact-results}

We treat factorized-contract training (\FACT{}) as a mechanism probe, not a
standalone optimizer or end-to-end generation method.

\paragraph{Intervention.}
After warm-up, \FACT{} replaces legacy classwise penalties with positive
null-excess constraints for conditional style MMD, conditional content MMD,
and within-class sampled-code HSIC, calibrated against matched prior--prior
banks or within-class permutations.  Sampled-style label HSIC, an adversarial
classifier, and posterior-mean HSIC target the remaining nonlinear information.
Content is stop-gradient for dependence terms, and direct joint MMD remains a
held-out outcome.  Appendix~\ref{app:fact} gives the exact objective,
optimization, calibration, and development history.  Raw/null calibration and
component construction use seed 0; the setting and seven gates are then fixed
before seeds 1--2, with training seeds separated from audit RNGs.

\paragraph{Fully traceable matched result.}
We report every retained control/\FACT{} pair for training seeds 0--2; seed 0
is the development pair and seeds 1--2 were run after the setting was fixed.
Joint MMD falls from $.002368\!\pm\!.000160$ to
$.001449\!\pm\!.000166$, a $38.89\!\pm\!3.65\%$ paired reduction; classwise
style--prior MMD falls from $.002567\!\pm\!.000153$ to
$.001902\!\pm\!.000148$.  Nevertheless, all three controls and all three
\FACT{} models reject joint equality ($p=.005$ in every aggregate test).
Style-probe accuracy falls from $76.91\%$ to $11.14\%$.  Decoder attribution
moves in the intended direction in every pair: content-donor following rises by
$25.41\!\pm\!8.05$ points and style-donor following falls by
$29.09\!\pm\!5.32$ points.  These gains carry a cost: global Gen-ACC falls by
$9.93\!\pm\!2.84$ points and reconstruction MAE worsens by
$6.52\!\pm\!5.57\%$.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{How to use the audit.}
The declared sampler determines the reference law.  Test $Q_y=P_y$ jointly,
localize rejection with the four clauses, then measure decoder/task effects.
The clauses point to the aggregate prior, style leakage, content sampler, and
residual dependence.  Decoder-insensitive mismatch may need no change; useful
label-bearing style may instead call for changing the sampler.

\paragraph{Equality versus practical compatibility.}
Equality rejection localizes failure; it is not a quality verdict.  Deployment
claims should pre-declare $(d,\epsilon)$ and report $\Delta_d$ with transport
and competence.  SCFlow's retrieval gain despite endpoint mismatch and
\FACT{}'s opposing MMD and Gen-ACC changes rule out a universal threshold;
cross-architecture tolerance calibration remains open.

\paragraph{Intervention scope.}
The three pairs support a stable direction for the chosen MNIST MMD and swap
metrics, not a general optimizer.  Kernel-free magnitude and competence do not
improve uniformly, so diagnostics require explicit transport and task constraints.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Shapes3D is IID, DRIT/DIVA are adapted, and SCFlow is a CLIP-space boundary
rather than a four-term natural-image audit.  Decoded natural-image and
continuous-condition evidence remains open.  \FACT{} is sequentially built on
MNIST from only three traceable pairs and needs factorial confirmation.

\section{Conclusion}
Factorized generation replaces the paired reconstruction law by an independent
latent law, while aggregate matching verifies only one of four requirements.
Mismatch can alter decoding, yet optimizing selected diagnostics can harm
generation; compatibility, transport, and competence must be evaluated separately.
