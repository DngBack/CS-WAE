\begin{abstract}
% ============================================================
A style variable in a factorized generative model is usually declared
class-invariant once its aggregate distribution matches a fixed prior,
$q(\zs)\approx\mathcal{N}(0,I)$, enforced by a \emph{global aggregate} loss
such as MMD. We argue this is the wrong criterion: it is a statement about
the marginal, while class-invariance is a statement about the conditional
$q(\zs\mid y{=}k)$, and matching the former does not imply the latter,
because a well-matched marginal is compatible with strongly class-structured
conditionals. We call the resulting failure \emph{conditional style leakage}:
the decoder exploits class information hidden in $\zs$, so class-conditional
generation (sampling $\zs$ independently of $\zc$) fails silently even while
reconstruction and marginal-MMD metrics look healthy.

We prove the gap formally and measure it across four unsupervised baselines
(VAE, WAE-MMD, $\beta$-TCVAE, FactorVAE) and our own \ours{} on MNIST: every
model shows near-zero global MMD alongside large inter-class style separation
$\Delta_{\mathrm{inter}}$ and linear-probe accuracy $74$--$100\%$ (chance
$10\%$). We propose \emph{per-class style MMD}, a supervised regularizer that
targets $q(\zs\mid y{=}k)$ directly, and a post-hoc \emph{class-conditional
style prior} that repairs generation without retraining.

Instantiated in \ours{}, per-class style MMD cuts $\Delta_{\mathrm{inter}}$ by
$67$--$77\%$ and raises MNIST naive-Gaussian generation self-accuracy from
$15\%$ to $70\%$ ($100\%$ when combined with the post-hoc prior), at a
clustering cost that is dataset-dependent: MNIST accuracy falls from
$99.15\%$ to $85.92\%$, while on CIFAR-10 the matched single-run comparison
changes by only $1.5$ points ($81.49\%\to80.00\%$; the 3-seed mean without
the regularizer is $80.87\pm0.52\%$). This asymmetry is itself a finding,
not just a caveat: the clustering cost of removing leakage tracks how much
class information the style variable was carrying in the first place, which
is dataset-dependent rather than a fixed property of the regularizer. Two
further results temper how far this fix can be trusted to generalize:
t-SNE visualizations make the leakage visually obvious for only one of the
four unsupervised baselines, despite the dependence being statistically real
in all four, and the class-conditional sampling recipe that reaches $100\%$
self-accuracy on MNIST reaches only $\sim\!70\%$ on CIFAR-10---the remedy
does not transfer across datasets as cleanly as the diagnostic that
motivated it. Architectural factorization does not imply statistical
independence, and supervising the dependence away is possible, but neither
free nor uniformly effective.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ============================================================

A factorized latent variable model is only as class-invariant as the
constraint it enforces, not the constraint it intends. The standard design
assigns class identity to a semantic variable $\zc$ and within-class
appearance to a style variable $\zs$, then regularizes $\zs$ toward a fixed
prior using a \emph{global aggregate} objective---MMD toward
$\mathcal{N}(0,I)$ \citep{tolstikhin2017wasserstein}, total correlation
penalties \citep{kim2018disentangling,chen2018isolating}, or adversarial
independence constraints \citep{chen2016infogan}---that operates on the
marginal $q(\zs)$. This equates a marginal-level statistic with a
conditional-level guarantee it does not imply: $q(\zs)\approx\mathcal{N}(0,I)$
is reachable by conditionals $q(\zs\mid y{=}k)$ that differ arbitrarily
across classes, so long as they average to a Gaussian. The factorization is
meant to support two goals---\emph{clustering}, by reading class structure
off $\zc$, and \emph{class-conditional generation}, by fixing $\zc$ to a
class prior while sampling $\zs$ freely---and it is the second goal that
this gap quietly breaks.

\paragraph{Conditional style leakage.}
We call the resulting failure \emph{conditional style leakage}. During
training the decoder sees $(\zc,\zs)$ pairs that always share class identity;
if $q(\zs\mid y{=}k)$ differs across classes, the decoder implicitly learns
class-specific $(\zc,\zs)$ interactions. At generation time, an independently
sampled $\zs$ forms a cross-class pair the decoder never saw, producing
semantically inconsistent output. The failure is \emph{silent} by
construction, not by bad luck: global MMD and reconstruction SSIM are
marginal- and posterior-manifold statistics respectively, and neither is
positioned to detect a conditional-level dependency, so both can look
healthy even when class-conditional generation is badly broken.

\paragraph{Scope.}
We measure this on four unsupervised baselines (VAE, WAE-MMD, $\beta$-TCVAE,
FactorVAE) and \ours{} on MNIST. All five show large inter-class style
separation despite near-zero marginal discrepancy. We are careful, however,
not to claim this comparison controls for architecture or training budget
(Section~\ref{sec:cross-model}): the baselines use a lighter encoder and a
shorter training schedule than \ours{}, so we treat the qualitative pattern,
not the ranking, as the evidence.

\paragraph{Remedies and their limits.}
\emph{Per-class style MMD} directly targets each $q(\zs\mid y{=}k)$. The
\emph{class-conditional style prior} is a post-hoc sampling correction. Both
help substantially, but neither is free or fully general: per-class style MMD
trades away clustering accuracy on MNIST (not on CIFAR-10), and the
class-conditional sampling recipe that perfectly repairs MNIST generation
only partially repairs CIFAR-10 generation (Section~\ref{sec:cifar-sampling}).

\paragraph{Case study: \ours{}.}
\ours{} (Factorized Class-Structured Spherical Cauchy Wasserstein
Auto-Encoder) separates the latent into a hyperspherical semantic variable
$\zc\in\Sph^{d_c-1}$ aligned with class-conditional Spherical Cauchy priors
and a Euclidean style variable $\zs\in\R^{d_s}$. Without per-class style MMD
it reaches $99.15\%$ clustering accuracy on MNIST but only $15\%$
class-conditional generation self-accuracy.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
Our contributions are fourfold, and each answers a question the previous one
raises. First, we formalize the gap between marginal matching and
conditional independence and prove it is unavoidable for any aggregate
divergence, not a defect of a particular loss function
(Proposition~\ref{prop:marginal-not-sufficient}). This raises an obvious
follow-up: is the gap only a theoretical curiosity, or does it bite in
practice? Second, we build a lightweight diagnostic and measure it across
five model families, showing the leakage is not only real but, for three of
the five, invisible to a naive 2D projection of the latent---the diagnostic
is doing work a plot cannot (Section~\ref{sec:cross-model}). Given that the
failure is real, the natural question is whether it is fixable. Third, we
propose per-class style MMD and show it substantially reduces, but does not
eliminate, leakage, at a clustering cost that is dataset-dependent rather
than uniform (Section~\ref{sec:method}). Finally, because a fix validated on
one dataset is not a fix validated in general, we test whether the same
remedy transfers: the class-conditional sampling recipe that is fully
effective on MNIST only partially repairs CIFAR-10, a limitation invisible
from the MNIST results alone (Section~\ref{sec:cifar-sampling}). We report
this negative transfer result rather than omit it, since it constrains how
far the proposed fix can be trusted to generalize.

\section{Related Work}
% ============================================================

\paragraph{Variational and Wasserstein auto-encoders.}
VAEs~\citep{kingma2013autoencoding} impose a per-example KL penalty toward a
fixed Gaussian prior. WAEs~\citep{tolstikhin2017wasserstein} relax this to a
global discrepancy between the aggregated posterior and the prior. MMD-based
WAEs use kernel two-sample tests~\citep{gretton2012kernel}; sliced-Wasserstein
AEs~\citep{kolouri2018sliced} use projected distances. All regularize the
\emph{marginal} of a latent variable, the setting where our gap applies.

\paragraph{Disentanglement and total correlation.}
$\beta$-VAE~\citep{higgins2017beta} increases the KL weight to encourage
disentanglement. $\beta$-TCVAE~\citep{chen2018isolating} and
FactorVAE~\citep{kim2018disentangling} decompose the KL into a total
correlation term and penalize it. InfoGAN~\citep{chen2016infogan} maximizes
mutual information between latent codes and images. These methods penalize
the joint or marginal distribution but do not explicitly target
$\zs\not\perp y$; Section~\ref{sec:cross-model} shows both still leak.

\paragraph{Conditional generative models and factorized latent spaces.}
Semi-supervised VAEs~\citep{kingma2014semi} and
CVAE~\citep{sohn2015learning} condition generation on labels but do not
study residual style independence. StyleGAN~\citep{karras2019style}
factorizes structure from style via a mapping network in a
class-unconditional regime. Our work addresses the label-guided setting
where clustering \emph{and} class-conditional generation are both required.

\paragraph{Deep generative clustering.}
DEC~\citep{xie2015unsupervised} and IDEC~\citep{guo2017improved} refine
cluster assignments in latent space; VaDE~\citep{jiang2016variational}
integrates a Gaussian-mixture prior into the generative model. None
diagnoses conditional style leakage, and the standard ACC/NMI/ARI protocol
would not reveal the generation failure we describe.

\paragraph{Hyperspherical latent variables.}
S-VAE~\citep{davidson2018hyperspherical} replaces the Gaussian posterior
with a von Mises-Fisher distribution. Spherical Cauchy
VAEs~\citep{sablica2025hyperspherical} provide a heavier-tailed alternative
with a M\"obius-transform reparameterization that \ours{} adopts. These
geometric choices are orthogonal to the leakage problem we study.

\section{The Conditional Style Leakage Problem}
\label{sec:phenomenon}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Setting and Notation}

Let $\mathcal{D}=\{(x_i,y_i)\}_{i=1}^n$ be a labeled dataset with $K$ classes.
A factorized model encodes each input into a semantic variable $\zc$ and a
style variable $\zs$. The style variable is regularized by
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{style}} = D\!\bigl(q(\zs),\;\mathcal{N}(0,I)\bigr),
  \label{eq:generic-style-reg}
\end{equation}
where $D$ is any aggregate divergence and $q(\zs)=\mathbb{E}_y[q(\zs\mid y)]$.
Class-conditional generation samples $\zs\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$ independently
of $\zc$.

\subsection{Formal Gap}
\label{sec:formal-gap}

\begin{proposition}[Marginal matching does not imply conditional independence]
\label{prop:marginal-not-sufficient}
Suppose $q(\zs\mid y{=}k)=\mathcal{N}(\mu_k,\Sigma_k)$ for $k=1,\dots,K$ with
$p(y)=\mathrm{Uniform}(\{1,\dots,K\})$, so
$q(\zs)=\frac{1}{K}\sum_k \mathcal{N}(\mu_k,\Sigma_k)$. Matching
$q(\zs)\approx\mathcal{N}(0,I)$ via any aggregate divergence is compatible
with \emph{arbitrary} class structure in the conditionals, provided the
mixture averages to a Gaussian: $q(\zs)\approx\mathcal{N}(0,I)$ does not
imply $q(\zs\mid y{=}k)\approx\mathcal{N}(0,I)$ for any $k$, nor
$\zs\perp y$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A mixture of Gaussians can approximate any distribution
\citep{goodfellow2016deep}, including $\mathcal{N}(0,I)$, with components of
arbitrary mean and covariance. Concretely, take $K=2$,
$\mu_1=-\mu_2=v\in\R^{d_s}$, $\Sigma_1=\Sigma_2=I-\tfrac12 vv^\top$: the
mixture mean is $0$ and its covariance approaches $I$ as $\|v\|$ grows
moderately, while the inter-class separation $2\|v\|$ is unbounded. A full
construction with $\Delta_{\mathrm{inter}}\to\infty$ under an exactly
matched marginal is given in Appendix~\ref{app:proof}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
A regularizer minimizing $D(q(\zs),\mathcal{N}(0,I))$ has zero gradient
incentive to reduce inter-class separation, since the mixture can match the
target while conditionals remain class-structured.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The Decoder Amplification Mechanism}
\label{sec:mechanism}

During training the decoder receives $(\zc,\zs)$ pairs from the same image;
both encode $y$ implicitly, $\zs$ because $q(\zs\mid y{=}k)$ is not
class-invariant. At generation time $\zc\sim p(\zc\mid y{=}k)$ encodes a
specific class while $\zs\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$ averages over all classes,
producing a cross-class pair never seen in training. Severity depends on how
class-structured $q(\zs\mid y)$ becomes: visually uniform classes (MNIST
digits) force more class information into $\zs$, amplifying the failure;
high-diversity classes (CIFAR-10) leave more class-neutral variation in
$\zs$.

\subsection{Diagnostic Metrics}
\label{sec:diagnostics}

\paragraph{Global MMD} $\MMD^2(q(\zs),\mathcal{N}(0,I))$: near zero signals
only that marginal matching succeeded.

\paragraph{Inter-class style separation}
$\Delta_{\mathrm{inter}}=\frac{1}{\binom{K}{2}}\sum_{j<k}\|\bar\mus^{(j)}-\bar\mus^{(k)}\|_2$,
$\bar\mus^{(k)}=\mathbb{E}[\mus\mid y{=}k]$: large values indicate
class-dependent style means despite small global MMD.

\paragraph{Linear probing accuracy $\mathrm{LP}(\zs\to y)$}: accuracy of a
linear classifier predicting $y$ from $\zs$; well above chance $1/K$
confirms recoverable class information.

\paragraph{HSIC$(\zs,y)$}: kernel-based dependence
measure~\citep{gretton2005measuring}; non-zero indicates $\zs\not\perp y$
regardless of marginal shape.

A model with near-zero global MMD but large $\Delta_{\mathrm{inter}}$,
elevated LP, or non-zero HSIC is exhibiting conditional style leakage.

\section{Cross-Model Validation}
\label{sec:cross-model}
% ============================================================

To test whether conditional style leakage is general rather than an artifact
of \ours{}'s design, we apply the diagnostic of
Section~\ref{sec:diagnostics} to four unsupervised baselines (VAE, WAE-MMD,
$\beta$-TCVAE, FactorVAE) and \ours{} on MNIST. These four models are
purpose-built for this diagnostic only, with a lightweight 3-layer
convolutional encoder trained for 100 epochs; they are \emph{not} the same
checkpoints as the like-named VAE/WAE-MMD entries used later for CIFAR-10
clustering comparison (Section~\ref{sec:cifar10}), which follow a different
protocol (Appendix~\ref{app:training}). \ours{}, by contrast, uses its full
ResNet-18 encoder trained for 300 epochs under the curriculum of
Section~\ref{sec:method}, so architecture and training budget are not
matched across the five rows of Table~\ref{tab:cross-model} and a reader
should not treat the $\Delta_{\mathrm{inter}}$ ranking between \ours{} and
the baselines as a controlled comparison. What the mismatch does not
undermine is the \emph{within-model} evidence: for every row individually,
global MMD is decoupled from $\Delta_{\mathrm{inter}}$ and LP regardless of
that row's encoder capacity or training length, which is the comparison the
diagnostic actually depends on.

All four baselines show LP far above chance ($74$--$93\%$) despite
near-zero global MMD. \ours{} shows the most extreme leakage
($\Delta_{\mathrm{inter}}=6.27$, LP$=100\%$), consistent with MNIST's low
intra-class diversity forcing more class information into $\zs$. WAE-MMD has
the largest global MMD among baselines ($0.0576$, $30$--$300\times$ the

the largest baseline $\Delta_{\mathrm{inter}}$ ($9.38$) and LP ($93\%$).

($2.30$, $2.27$), but LP stays far above chance ($74$--$76\%$): penalizing
joint/marginal statistics reduces but does not eliminate the dependence.

($6.27\to1.46$) and LP by 54 points ($100\%\to45.6\%$)---still far above
chance, and higher than three of the four unsupervised baselines. Naive
self-accuracy rises correspondingly from $0.15$ to $0.70$.

\paragraph{Is the leakage visible without the diagnostic?}
A natural question is whether Table~\ref{tab:cross-model} is telling us
something a simple visualization would already show. Figure~\ref{fig:tsne}
projects $\zs$ (2000 test points) with t-SNE, colored by class, for all six
rows. \emph{Read this figure the opposite way from a typical clustering
figure}: $\zs$ is supposed to be class-\emph{invariant}, so a well-mixed,
color-blended cloud is the desired (good) outcome and tight,
color-separated clusters are the failure mode (high leakage), consistent
with the direction of $\Delta_{\mathrm{inter}}$ in
Table~\ref{tab:cross-model}. By that reading, the answer is mixed: WAE-MMD
and \ours{} (no per-class MMD)---the two rows with the \emph{largest}
$\Delta_{\mathrm{inter}}$, i.e.\ the two worst rows in
Table~\ref{tab:cross-model}---show clearly separated clusters, and
\ours{}\,+\,per-class MMD (lower $\Delta_{\mathrm{inter}}$, i.e.\ improved)
shows visibly less separated, more mixed clusters, matching the table.
VAE, $\beta$-TCVAE, and FactorVAE show \emph{no} visually obvious clustering
at all, despite LP accuracies of $74$--$87\%$ in the same latent space. This
is itself evidence for our methodological point: for three of the five
baseline-scale models, marginal inspection or a naive 2D projection would
miss the leakage entirely, and only the quantitative diagnostic (a linear
probe trained on the full $64$-dimensional $\zs$) recovers it.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Problem Setting: Label-Guided Generative Clustering}

Given labeled images $\mathcal{D}=\{(x_i,y_i)\}_{i=1}^n$ with $K$ classes, we
want (1) reconstruction that preserves visual content, (2) clustering that
recovers class structure from semantic codes without using labels at
evaluation time, and (3) class-conditional generation that produces
class-consistent images from class priors.

\subsection{Factorized Architecture: \ours{}}

A shared ResNet-18 backbone extracts $h=f_\phi(x)$, followed by a 256-unit
FC layer with SiLU, then two heads:
\begin{equation}
  E_\phi(x) = \bigl(\tilde{\muc},\;r_c,\;\mus,\;\log\sigma_s^2\bigr).
\end{equation}
The semantic direction is $\ell_2$-normalized with bounded concentration:
\begin{equation}
  \muc = \frac{\tilde{\muc}}{\|\tilde{\muc}\|_2},
  \qquad
  \rho_c = \sigma(r_c)(1-\varepsilon),
  \label{eq:semantic-head}
\end{equation}
sampled via the Spherical Cauchy M\"obius
reparameterization~\citep{sablica2025hyperspherical}:
\begin{equation}
  \eta_c\sim\mathrm{Unif}(\Sph^{d_c-1}),
  \qquad
  \zc = T_{\muc,\rho_c}(\eta_c).
  \label{eq:sc-reparam}
\end{equation}
The style latent uses a Gaussian reparameterization
$\zs=\mus+\sigma_s\odot\varepsilon_s$, $\varepsilon_s\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$.
Both are concatenated and decoded, $\hat{x}=D_\theta([\zc;\zs])$, by a
residual upsampling decoder ($512{\to}256{\to}128{\to}64$ channels,
$4\times4\to32\times32$). In all experiments $d_c=64$, $d_s=128$
(Appendix~\ref{app:arch}).

\subsection{Class-Conditional Semantic Prior}

For each class $k$, \ours{} maintains a center $m_k\in\Sph^{d_c-1}$ with
prior $p(\zc\mid y{=}k)=\SCauchy(m_k,\rho_p)$, $\rho_p=0.7$, updated by EMA
from batch class-mean directions once per epoch:
\begin{equation}
  m_k \leftarrow \mathrm{normalize}\!\bigl(\tau\,m_k + (1-\tau)\,\bar\muc^{(k)}\bigr),
  \quad \tau=0.95.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Loss Terms}
\label{sec:losses}

\begin{align}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rec}} &= \lambda_1\|x-\hat{x}\|_1 + \lambda_{\mathrm{LPIPS}}\,\mathrm{LPIPS}(x,\hat{x}), \\
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{class}} &= \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\MMD^2(Q_k,P_k), \\
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{agg}} &= \MMD^2\!\bigl(\{\zc^i\},\,\{z_{c,r_j}^p\}\bigr), \\
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{style}} &= \MMD_{\mathrm{RBF}}\!\bigl(\{\zs^i\},\,\{\varepsilon_j\}\bigr), \label{eq:style-mmd}\\
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{style\text{-}cls}} &= \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}
    \MMD_{\mathrm{RBF}}\!\bigl(\{\zs^i{:}y_i{=}k\},\{\varepsilon_j\}_{|y_i=k|}\bigr), \label{eq:style-cls-mmd}\\
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cls}} &= \CE(h(\muc),\,y),
\end{align}
where $Q_k=\{\zc^i:y_i=k\}$, $P_k\sim p(\zc\mid y{=}k)$, and
$\lambda_1{=}1.0$, $\lambda_{\mathrm{LPIPS}}{=}0.1$.
Eq.~\ref{eq:style-mmd} aligns the marginal style distribution but, as shown
in Section~\ref{sec:phenomenon}, does not prevent conditional leakage;
Eq.~\ref{eq:style-cls-mmd} is our proposed fix, targeting each conditional
$q(\zs\mid y{=}k)$ directly. The total objective is
\begin{align}
  \mathcal{L} &= \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rec}} + \alpha(t)\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{class}}
   + \beta(t)\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{agg}} + \gamma(t)\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{style}} \nonumber\\
  &\quad + \delta(t)\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{style\text{-}cls}} + \eta(t)\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cls}},
  \label{eq:total}
\end{align}
with time-varying coefficients over a four-phase, 300-epoch curriculum:
(A, 0--49) reconstruction only; (B, 50--99) ramp $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{style}}$,
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{style\text{-}cls}}$, $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cls}}$, so
per-class style regularization begins before the decoder can learn to exploit
class structure in $\zs$; (C, 100--199) ramp $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{class}}$,
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{agg}}$ to half weight; (D, 200--299) ramp both to full
weight (exact schedule in Appendix~\ref{app:schedule}).

\subsection{Inference and Generation}

\paragraph{Clustering.} K-means on deterministic $\muc$, Hungarian-matched
to compute ACC/NMI/ARI; labels are not used.

\paragraph{Naive prior sampling.} $\zc\sim p(\zc\mid y{=}k)$,
$\zs\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$. Reliable only when per-class style MMD is used.

\paragraph{Class-conditional style prior (post-hoc).} Without per-class
style MMD, estimate $\bar\mus^{(k)}=\mathbb{E}[\mus\mid y{=}k]$,
$\bar\sigma_s^{2(k)}=\mathrm{Var}[\mus\mid y{=}k]$ from encoded training
data, then sample $\zs\sim\mathcal{N}(\bar\mus^{(k)},\tau^2\diag(\bar\sigma_s^{2(k)}))$
with $\tau=0.25$. On MNIST this restores generation self-accuracy from
$15\%$ to $100\%$ on an existing checkpoint with no retraining
(Section~\ref{sec:sampling}); Section~\ref{sec:cifar-sampling} shows this
recipe is only partially effective on CIFAR-10.

\label{sec:cross-dataset}

diagnostic (Section~\ref{sec:diagnostics}) on the $\delta{=}0$ \ours{}
checkpoint for all three datasets.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{crossdataset_leakage.png}
\caption{Left: $\Delta_{\mathrm{inter}}$ and linear-probe accuracy across
         datasets, ordered by increasing intra-class visual diversity---both
         decrease monotonically, matching the hypothesis. Right: naive-Gaussian
         generation self-accuracy does \emph{not} follow the same order
         (CIFAR-10 is lowest, not highest); we attribute this to CIFAR-10
         classification being intrinsically harder for the auxiliary

         leakage-severity proxy (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).}
\label{fig:crossdataset}
\end{figure}

$\Delta_{\mathrm{inter}}$ and LP support the hypothesis cleanly: MNIST
($6.27$, $100.0\%$) $>$ Fashion-MNIST ($4.86$, $97.7\%$) $>$ CIFAR-10
($4.19$, $89.9\%$). Naive-Gaussian generation self-accuracy does not:
MNIST $0.15$, Fashion-MNIST $0.17$, CIFAR-10 $0.12$---CIFAR-10 is lowest, not
highest. The two families of metrics differ in what they are computed on,
and that difference explains the disagreement rather than undermining
either measurement. $\Delta_{\mathrm{inter}}$ and LP operate directly on
$\zs$ and therefore isolate the dependency we care about. Self-accuracy is
measured on the generated \emph{image}, mediated by an auxiliary classifier,
so it additionally inherits how confusable the real classes already are
under blurry generation---a dataset-difficulty effect with nothing to do
with conditional style leakage, and one that CIFAR-10 has on top of leakage.
We see no principled reason to discard the metric that disagrees, and doing
so would let us report a cleaner story than the data supports; the
disagreement instead tells us that generation self-accuracy, on its own, is
not a reliable cross-dataset proxy for leakage severity, even though it
remains the right metric for asking whether a specific checkpoint's
generation is repaired (Section~\ref{sec:sampling}).

$1.5$ points ($0.8149\to0.8000$, within normal seed variance;
Table~\ref{tab:cifar-main}), $\Delta_{\mathrm{inter}}$ drops $67\%$, and
naive-sampling FID improves ($83.59\to74.42$). On MNIST the leakage
reduction is larger in relative terms ($77\%$) but clustering accuracy drops
substantially ($99.15\%\to85.92\%$). We interpret this as evidence that on
MNIST---where classes are visually uniform---the style variable had been
carrying class-discriminative information the semantic variable did not
fully capture; suppressing it via $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{style\text{-}cls}}$
removes a source of leakage but also removes a signal the K-means-on-$\muc$
pipeline was implicitly benefiting from.

Per-class style MMD alone does not close the gap: naive-sampling
self-accuracy on the $\delta{=}1$ MNIST checkpoint is $70\%$
(Table~\ref{tab:cross-model}), not $100\%$. Reaching $100\%$ requires
\emph{combining} both remedies---applying the post-hoc class-conditional
prior ($\tau{=}0.25$) on top of the $\delta{=}1$ checkpoint, rather than
either remedy alone---which we verified directly on this checkpoint (naive
$0.70\to$ class-conditional $1.00$, same protocol as
Table~\ref{tab:sampling}). We read this as showing the two remedies are
complementary rather than redundant: per-class style MMD reduces how much
class information $\zs$ carries, and the post-hoc prior corrects for what
remains.

% ============================================================
\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
% ============================================================

\paragraph{Why CIFAR-10 is milder than MNIST, with a caveat.}
CIFAR-10's higher intra-class visual diversity weakens conditional style
dependence, and $\Delta_{\mathrm{inter}}$/LP confirm this cleanly
(Section~\ref{sec:cross-dataset}). But Section~\ref{sec:cifar-sampling}
shows the practical remedy does not inherit this cleanly: the
class-conditional Gaussian prior that perfectly fixes MNIST generation only
partially fixes CIFAR-10 generation, and CIFAR-10 samples remain visibly
blurry (FID $\sim\!83$--$84$, well above photorealistic ranges). We avoid
describing CIFAR-10 naive-sampling output as ``visually plausible''; at this
FID the more accurate description is ``recognizable but not sharp,'' and
under the $\tau{=}0.25$ class-conditional prior several classes are not
reliably identifiable by eye despite scoring well on the auxiliary
classifier (Figure~\ref{fig:cifar-sampling-grid}).

\paragraph{Global vs.\ conditional regularization.}
Global aggregate matching is standard in WAEs and disentanglement models;
our results show it is insufficient whenever the style variable must be
class-invariant for generation. Per-class style MMD is a supervised analogue
of total correlation penalties, targeting the single dependency
$\zs\not\perp y$ directly.

\paragraph{The reconstruction--generation gap.}
The MNIST checkpoint reaches SSIM $0.983$ yet FID $73.99$ under naive prior
sampling (Figure~\ref{fig:recon-vs-gen}). Reconstruction quality measures
fidelity on the posterior manifold, not the prior; evaluation of generative
clustering models should report both.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

We list what this paper does \emph{not} establish, rather than leave it
implicit. (1) Baseline comparisons (Tables~\ref{tab:cifar-baselines},
\ref{tab:mnist}) and Fashion-MNIST (Table~\ref{tab:fashion-mnist}) are
single-seed; we do not report multi-seed variance we have not measured.
(2) The clustering-leakage trade-off (Figure~\ref{fig:tradeoff}) is two
points per dataset, not a continuous curve; we did not train intermediate
$\delta$ values. (3) The cross-model comparison
(Section~\ref{sec:cross-model}) does not control for encoder capacity or
training budget between \ours{} and the four baselines. (4) We do not
report a component-wise ablation of \ours{} (e.g., removing the semantic
variable, the class MMD term, or varying the prior geometry) beyond the
$\delta$ (per-class style MMD) axis, since we do not have completed runs for
those variants. (5) The class-conditional style prior's failure to fully
transfer to CIFAR-10 (Section~\ref{sec:cifar-sampling}) is measured on one
checkpoint per dataset; we have not tested whether a richer conditional
(e.g., a per-class mixture rather than a single Gaussian) closes the gap.

% ============================================================
\section{Conclusion}
% ============================================================

Conditional style leakage is a failure mode in factorized generative models:
global aggregate regularization of a style variable does not prevent it from
becoming class-dependent, because marginal matching is necessary but not
sufficient for conditional independence. We proved this gap, measured it
with a three-metric diagnostic across five model families, and showed it is
statistically real even when not visually obvious. Per-class style MMD cuts
inter-class style separation by $67$--$77\%$ and raises MNIST naive-sampling
self-accuracy from $15\%$ to $70\%$ ($100\%$ combined with the post-hoc
style prior); the clustering cost is negligible on CIFAR-10 but substantial
on MNIST. Critically, the remedy that fully repairs MNIST generation only
partially repairs CIFAR-10 generation, indicating that fixing the diagnostic
finding does not automatically fix the underlying generative gap on more
diverse data. Factorized generative models should verify class-invariance of
style variables via conditional diagnostics, not marginal statistics alone,
and should report where proposed remedies do and do not generalize.

\section{Architecture Details}
\label{app:arch}

The ResNet-18 backbone for $32\times32$ inputs replaces the first
$7\times7$ convolution (stride 2) with a $3\times3$ convolution (stride 1,
padding 1) and removes the max-pool layer, yielding a $4\times4$ spatial
output with 512 channels. The shared FC layer maps
$512\times4\times4{=}8{,}192$ to 256 dimensions with SiLU. Semantic and
style heads are separate linear layers from this 256-dimensional
intermediate. The decoder uses three ResBlockUp stages (bilinear
$2\times$ upsample, $1\times1$ projection, GroupNorm+SiLU+Conv residual
block), followed by a $3\times3$ output convolution and sigmoid.

\section{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:marginal-not-sufficient}}
\label{app:proof}

Let $K{=}10$, $d_s{=}2$, $q(\zs\mid y{=}k)=\mathcal{N}(\mu_k,\Sigma_k)$ with
$\mu_k=r(\cos(2\pi k/K),\sin(2\pi k/K))^\top$ for radius $r{>}0$ and
$\Sigma_k=I-\frac{r^2}{1+r^2}\hat\mu_k\hat\mu_k^\top$,
$\hat\mu_k=\mu_k/\|\mu_k\|$. The mixture mean is
$\frac{1}{K}\sum_k\mu_k=0$ and the mixture covariance is
$I+\frac{r^2}{K}\sum_k\hat\mu_k\hat\mu_k^\top-\frac{r^2}{K}I$, which
approaches $I$ for this symmetric configuration. The inter-class mean
separation is $\Delta_{\mathrm{inter}}\approx r\sqrt2$, unbounded as
$r\to\infty$, while the marginal stays $\approx\mathcal{N}(0,I)$: this
shows $\Delta_{\mathrm{inter}}$ can be arbitrarily large even under an
exactly matched marginal, proving the proposition.
